The quality of your Company Facts directly determines the quality of your AI output. Vague facts produce vague responses — if you tell the AI "we care about customers", it can't do much with that. Specific, well-written facts produce on-brand, accurate, and immediately useful responses. Investing ten minutes in writing a sharp fact will save your team hours of correcting AI drafts.

## The Golden Rules

Follow these five principles every time you write a new Company Fact.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Be specific, not general">
    Concrete facts give AI tools something to work with. "Our pricing starts at €89/month for the Pro plan" is actionable. "We have competitive pricing" is noise. When in doubt, ask yourself: could a new hire act on this fact alone? If not, make it more specific.
  </Step>
  <Step title="One fact per entry">
    Don't bundle multiple pieces of information into a single fact. If you're describing a product feature and its pricing in the same entry, split them. Focused facts are retrieved more reliably and are easier to update when something changes.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Write in the present tense">
    Facts describe your company as it is right now. Use present tense throughout: "Our refund policy is 30 days, no questions asked" — not "Our refund policy was updated to 30 days." If the situation changes, update the fact.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Use the language AI should mirror">
    AI tools will reflect the vocabulary in your facts back to users. If you want the AI to refer to customers as "clients", use "clients" in the fact. If you use a product name like "Brain Sync", spell it exactly the way you want it to appear in AI output.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Include edge cases">
    Policy facts are most valuable when they cover exceptions. "Payment terms are net-30 for new customers; net-15 for accounts over 90 days past due" gives the AI the nuance it needs to handle real situations — not just the default case.
  </Step>
</Steps>

    Use these examples as a template for writing facts about what your company sells.

    ```
    Title:    Product Overview — Company Brain
    Category: Products & Services
    Content:  Company Brain is an AI context layer that stores a company's
              facts, rules, brand voice, and strategy in one place. It
              delivers that context automatically to AI tools via the Model
              Context Protocol (MCP), so every session starts with full
              company knowledge — no copy-pasting required.
    ```

    ```
    Title:    Key Feature — Automated Fact Updates
    Category: Products & Services
    Content:  Pro and Team plans include automated agents that monitor
              designated sources (docs, Notion pages, internal wikis) and
              draft new or updated facts for Curator review. Teams receive
              a weekly digest summarising all knowledge base changes.
    ```

    ```
    Title:    Pricing — Pro Plan
    Category: Products & Services
    Content:  The Pro plan costs €89/month per company (not per seat). It
              includes up to 2,000 Company Facts, 5 Curator seats, automated
              fact updates, and MCP access for the entire team.
    ```
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Brand & Communication">
    Use these examples to encode your tone, style, and communication preferences.

    ```
    Title:    Tone of Voice
    Category: Brand & Communication
    Content:  Our tone is professional but approachable. We write in plain
              language, avoid jargon, and never talk down to the reader.
              We're confident without being arrogant. We use "we" and "you"
              to stay conversational and direct.
    ```

    ```
    Title:    Words to Avoid
    Category: Brand & Communication
    Content:  Do not use: "synergy", "leverage" (as a verb), "best-in-class",
              "world-class", "cutting-edge", or "disruptive". These terms are
              overused and vague. Replace them with specific, evidence-backed
              claims about what we actually do.
    ```

    ```
    Title:    How to Address Customers
    Category: Brand & Communication
    Content:  Address customers by their first name in all written
              communications. Use "you" and "your" rather than "the customer"
              or "users". In formal documents such as contracts, use the
              company name on first reference and "you" thereafter.
    ```
  </Tab>

    ```
    Title:    Discount Approval Thresholds
    Category: Policies & Rules
    Content:  Discounts up to 10% can be approved by any account executive.
              Discounts between 10% and 20% require sales manager approval.

              discounts must be logged in the CRM before the quote is sent.
    ```

    ```
    Title:    Proposal Discount Rules — End of Quarter
    Category: Policies & Rules
    Content:  During the final two weeks of each quarter, account executives

              up to a maximum combined discount of 15%. This exception applies
              to new business only, not renewals or expansions.
    ```

## What Makes a Bad Fact?

Avoid these common mistakes when building your knowledge base.

<Accordion title="Too vague to act on">
  **Bad:** "We care about quality."

  This tells the AI nothing it can apply. Every company claims to care about quality.

  **Better:** "Every client deliverable goes through peer review before it's sent. The reviewer checks for accuracy, tone consistency, and alignment with the client's stated goals. Deliverables that don't pass review are returned to the author with written feedback."

  Specific facts produce specific, useful AI output. If a fact wouldn't help a new hire do their job, it won't help an AI either.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Too long — needs splitting">
  If your fact requires more than three sentences, it's probably trying to do too much at once. Long facts are harder for AI tools to retrieve accurately and harder for Curators to review and maintain.

  Split long facts into focused single-topic entries. A fact about your refund policy should cover only your refund policy — not your refund policy, your payment terms, and your late-payment process all in one block.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Out of date">
  Stale facts are worse than no facts. If the AI confidently applies a policy that changed six months ago, it can mislead customers or create internal confusion.

  Assign a Curator to each category who is responsible for keeping those facts current. Use the review workflow to trigger a check whenever a policy or product changes. Pro and Team plan users get automated agents that flag potential staleness based on source monitoring.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Written for humans, not AI retrieval">
  Facts like "See the pricing page for details" or "As discussed in the all-hands meeting" are not self-contained. AI tools cannot follow links or recall meetings. Every fact must be fully self-explanatory — it should convey the knowledge it encodes without relying on any external reference.
</Accordion>

## Where to Start

<Tip>
  Start with the ten facts you find yourself copy-pasting into AI chats the most. These are almost certainly your highest-value facts — the context that AI tools need most often and that you're currently re-explaining from scratch in every session. Get those ten facts written, reviewed, and Live before expanding your knowledge base further.
</Tip>
